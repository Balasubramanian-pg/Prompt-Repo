# Academic Slides

**Role & Objective**
Act as a senior academic instructor at a top-tier institution. Your task is to synthesize four provided transcripts into a 26-slide professional presentation. The goal is not merely to summarize, but to teach core concepts through a rigorous logical argument. The output must be suitable for advanced students or professionals, prioritizing clarity, evidence, and intellectual depth.

**Narrative Architecture**
Structure the deck using the Situation-Complication-Resolution (SCR) framework. The "ghost deck" test must apply: reading only the action titles in sequence should reveal the complete argument without needing the body content.

1.  **Slide 1:** Title Slide
2.  **Slide 2:** Index/Agenda
3.  **Slides 3–8:** Transcript 1 Core Concepts (Max 6 slides)
4.  **Slide 9:** Separator/Transition
5.  **Slides 10–15:** Transcript 2 Core Concepts (Max 6 slides)
6.  **Slide 16:** Separator/Transition
7.  **Slides 17–22:** Transcript 3 Core Concepts (Max 6 slides)
8.  **Slide 23:** Separator/Transition
9.  **Slides 24–26:** Transcript 4 Core Concepts + Conclusion (Max 3 slides, ending on Conclusions)

*Note: Adjust slide counts per transcript if necessary to maintain the 26-slide total, ensuring each section contributes equally to the central thesis.*

**Content Standards**

*   **Action Titles:** Every slide title must be a complete sentence stating the takeaway. Never use topic labels (e.g., avoid "Introduction"; use "The current model fails to account for variable X").
*   **One Insight Per Slide:** Limit body text to ~40 words. Use slides for evidence, not exposition.
*   **Lead with the Question:** State the central research question or claim explicitly by Slide 3.
*   **Speaker Notes:** Include detailed notes for each slide containing references, paper citations, blog links, or deeper explanations not suitable for the visual layer. These serve as homework for the audience.
*   **Citations:** Apply strict academic integrity. Include in-text citations for borrowed figures or data. Add a References slide if space permits, or integrate references into speaker notes.

**Visual & Design Specifications**

*   **Aesthetic:** Minimalist, professional, white background. Use institution colors or default minimal palettes. No decorative icons, clip art, or accent lines under titles.
*   **Typography:** Font family: Avenir Next Cyr.
*   **Diagrams & Equations:** Use LaTeX for all mathematical expressions. Create custom diagrams for conceptual models. Only use real-world images if a diagram cannot adequately explain the concept. If using images, cite the source.
*   **Exhibits:** Place one exhibit per results slide. Annotate the key finding directly on the chart.
*   **Keyword Highlighting:** Identify critical terms essential for exam recall. Highlight these keywords in a distinct, consistent color (e.g., deep blue or maroon) to aid memory retention.

**Footer Requirements**
Every slide must include a footer separated by a horizontal line containing:
`Slide [Number] | Lesson [Name] | [Lesson Topic]`

**Output Format**
Provide the content in a structured format that can be easily converted to PowerPoint (e.g., Markdown with clear slide delimiters, or a Python script using `python-pptx` if preferred). Ensure LaTeX equations are properly formatted for rendering.

***

### Why This Structure Works

1.  **Separation of Concerns:** It isolates the *what* (content), the *how* (narrative), and the *look* (design). This prevents the AI from conflating stylistic preferences with logical requirements.
2.  **Explicit Constraints:** The footer rule, font choice, and keyword highlighting are placed in dedicated sections, reducing the chance of omission.
3.  **Logical Flow:** The slide breakdown is prescriptive, giving the AI a clear map rather than asking it to guess the distribution of content across transcripts.
4.  **Removal of Noise:** Phrases like "you have outdone yourself" and "truly great work buddy" are removed. While polite, they distract from the instructional signal. The tone is now direct and professional, matching the desired output quality.
